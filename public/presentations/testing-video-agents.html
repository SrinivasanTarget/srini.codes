08 hard problem 01

The audio was there. It was also silence

what we captured
RMS 0.2
what was there
RMS 3,400
nothing renders
→
zeros, forever
forced render, gain 0
→
signal
Assert on signal, not on status. “No error” proves nothing.
Srini. Set it up: we joined the call, we got a remote audio track, we read thousands of frames off it, every single one was digital silence. Worse than "no output", the pipeline reports perfect health and delivers frames on schedule. Cause: Chromium only pulls samples through a track something is actually rendering, and a reader on a track nobody plays gets zeros forever without ever erroring. What failed: cloning the track and recording it, and a muted audio element that autoplay policy quietly declines. What works: route every tapped track through a Web Audio graph at gain zero so the render thread always pulls, read decoded frames with MediaStreamTrackProcessor, watchdog all zero frames after four seconds. Best line: we had to tap every inbound track, because the agent was on the second one.

“mhm” under 0.25s: absorbed 0.5s or more: yield the floor end of turn 1.1s
Silence is not the end of a turn. It is a comma.
Sai. Empathy line: everyone has been on a laggy video call and started talking at the same time. We had to encode that social skill as a threshold and thresholds alone cannot do it. Our first version cut people off, the fix made it wait so long the interviewer said "take your time, when you are ready", which is a machine politely telling you your machine is broken. The answer is a confirm then yield debounce: detect the interrupt, wait 250 milliseconds, check they are still talking. Backchannels get absorbed, real barge in still works. How we knew: an A B run on identical recorded audio, instrumented to separate "interrupt signal fired" from "floor actually yielded".

Srini. Deliberate trap: let them read the bars and assume v2 wins, then land the last line. This is time from the agent finishing its question to the first sound out of our candidate's mouth, measured on live runs. The relatable mistake: our first filler fired after transcription, so it masked nothing, because transcription was the 1.9 seconds. Srini. The slide senior people remember. A mixed mono track cannot show you an overlap, only a diarised transcript or an ear can. Generalise it: when your metric aggregates across the thing that fails, it will report health forever. That applies to flaky test dashboards and p50 latency exactly as much as it applies to audio.

If you could not confirm it by watching, neither can the grader.
Sai takes this section, about 5 minutes. Frame it first: you have a two minute video of two machines talking, there is no expected output, and somebody still has to say pass or fail and defend it. Then this slide. Tell them to photograph it, it is the most immediately useful slide for a working tester. The rule: one observable behaviour per criterion, and if it contains the word "and" it is probably two. Friendly is not observable, a greeting is.

Sai. Click to timestamp is the feature that converts sceptics: it turns "the AI says it failed" into "watch it fail at 1:24". Then walk the four chips as the rules behind a verdict. Dwell on the last one: any AI grading tool can quietly launder its own failures into customer failures, so a separate inconclusive state is a deliberate and slightly painful design choice, and it is the credibility argument. Frame all four as: we chose to look worse on the dashboard in exchange for a number you can act on. Mention the four diagnostic pillars only if there is time, pillars explain a result, criteria decide it.
